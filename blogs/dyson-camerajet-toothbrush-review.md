## Key Features

Before weighing the claims below, here is what CameraJet does, according to Dyson's own descriptions:

- **Built-in camera:** A 100,000-pixel macro lens captures close-up images of your teeth as you brush.
- **AI gap detection:** Machine-learning software (Dyson calls it Gap Optical Targeting) analyzes roughly 28 images per second to spot gaps between teeth in real time.
- **Conical liquid jet:** The brush head can release a short, targeted burst of mouthrinse toward a detected gap.
- **Sonic oscillation:** In its variable-oscillation modes, the brush head can move up to about 58,000 times per minute for standard cleaning.
- **App connectivity:** The MyDyson app can show live brushing footage and track cleaning statistics over time.

These are manufacturer specifications, not independent test results — the sections below look at what they add up to in practice. [Source: Dyson CameraJet product page](https://www.dyson.com/oral-care/electric-toothbrush/camerajet/ceramic-pink-set) and [launch announcement](https://www.dyson.com/discover/news/latest/introducing-camerajet).

The Dyson CameraJet toothbrush combines brushing with a camera-guided liquid jet. Before spending nearly $500, it is worth asking what that combination adds to your daily routine. Its features are unusual, but the published comparisons leave important questions about its value unanswered.

The reason for caution is the gap between an impressive feature and evidence that it improves everyday care. Before buying, look at what was tested, what the product was compared with, and whether the extra steps fit your habits.

## What is the Dyson CameraJet toothbrush?

Dyson introduced CameraJet on September 1, 2026. According to the company, a small camera identifies spaces between teeth and triggers a jet of mouthrinse while you brush. The device also offers app-based guidance and live camera viewing. These are manufacturer descriptions; no hands-on testing was done for this article. [Source: Dyson launch announcement](https://www.dyson.com/discover/news/latest/introducing-camerajet).

Combining two steps could appeal to someone who regularly brushes but skips cleaning between teeth. Whether it actually makes that person's routine easier is a practical question a specification sheet cannot answer.

## Read the cleaning claims with their limits

Dyson advertises up to 69% more plaque removal than market-leading premium electric toothbrushes. Its footnote identifies a laboratory comparison in hard-to-reach areas, using brush-only Variable mode. That is not a finding of 69% fewer cavities in people.

The company also cites a six-week clinical study involving 204 participants, comparing twice-daily Brush and Auto-jet use with a manual toothbrush. This is a different comparison from the electric-toothbrush laboratory test. [Source: Dyson product page, footnotes 2 and 4](https://www.dyson.com/oral-care/electric-toothbrush/camerajet/ceramic-pink-set).

Neither description answers whether CameraJet outperforms a good electric brush **plus daily cleaning between teeth** over the long term. That is the comparison a buyer already following that routine would want to see.

This article assesses the manufacturer's published summaries, without an independent evaluation of the full study reports. Those summaries offer a starting point for judging the claims, but they cannot settle every question about long-term results. Their limits also do not establish that the toothbrush is ineffective or unsafe.

## Can it replace flossing?

Cleaning between teeth remains part of daily care. The American Dental Association explains that a toothbrush alone cannot fully clean those spaces and describes several options, including string floss, small interdental brushes, and water flossers. Water flossers can help people who find other tools difficult to use. [Source: ADA flossing guidance](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/flossing).

That supports considering a liquid-cleaning tool. It does not establish that this particular device replaces every tool for every mouth. Ask your dentist or hygienist which method fits your tooth spacing and dental work before dropping a routine they have recommended.

## What does the price buy you?

Dyson's U.S. Ceramic Pink listing showed $499.99 on September 11, 2026. The company specifies genuine replacement heads and recommends non-foaming toothpaste and mouthrinse for best performance. Check ongoing supply costs as well as the purchase price. [Source: Dyson product details and FAQs](https://www.dyson.com/oral-care/electric-toothbrush/camerajet/ceramic-pink-set).

The useful question is what problem you need to solve. If holding floss is difficult, ask about easier ways to clean between teeth. If you tend to rush brushing, consider whether guidance would help you slow down. If your current routine is comfortable and your dental team is satisfied with your cleaning, a new camera feature alone is a weak reason to replace it.

Before deciding, consider:

- **Daily effort:** Read the filling and cleaning instructions. Would you keep doing those steps after the novelty wears off?
- **Comfort:** Ask about a suitable brush head and technique, especially if you have sensitive areas.
- **Total cost:** Price the supplies you expect to use over a year.
- **Evidence:** Look for the comparison behind a claim, not just the largest percentage on the page.

## The basics still matter more than the feature list

The ADA says both manual and powered toothbrushes can clean effectively. Its advice is to use soft bristles, brush gently for two minutes twice a day, and use fluoride toothpaste. [Source: ADA toothbrush guidance](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/toothbrushes).

An expensive toothbrush does not remove those responsibilities. Choose equipment that helps you follow them consistently, along with the daily cleaning between teeth that suits your mouth.

## Is the Dyson CameraJet worth buying?

CameraJet may appeal to someone who wants guided brushing and liquid cleaning in one device. The case for spending more depends on whether those features solve a problem you actually have, and whether you are comfortable with the cost and upkeep.

For someone already brushing thoroughly and cleaning between teeth each day, the comparisons described above do not establish a clear reason to switch. Before upgrading, ask your dental team where your current routine falls short. Start with that answer, then choose a tool that helps address it.
